# Work log, 26 November 2026

The first day the Wio-E5 star network ran on real hardware: a second gateway (995A) with a Wio-E5 on its USB port, a laptop test node, a range walk, and the first C6 field node on the star (7A44). It ends with the device settings as left, the open items, and a checklist for the outdoor test. Reference material is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) §13.

## Background (24–25 November)

**24 Nov.** `shared/wio.c` written: a star-network radio driver for the Seeed Wio-E5 (STM32WLE5) on its factory AT firmware, behind the same interface as the E52 driver, so LMESH, MQTT, Modbus and firmware updates run unchanged. It is selected at build time (`CONFIG_LMESH_RADIO_WIO`). Bench tests with two Wio-E5 minis (A and B), raw AT test mode, no protocol, 115200 baud:

| Spreading factor, 500 kHz | 16-byte ping-pong, median | Longer frame, median |
|---|---|---|
| SF5 | 226 ms | |
| SF7 | 246 ms | 519 ms (200 B) |
| SF9 | 310 ms | |
| SF10 | 402 ms | |
| SF12 | 876 ms | 2,084 ms (100 B) |

The AT firmware adds about 100 ms to every transmission, at every spreading factor. That, not airtime, sets the pace of short exchanges; firmware of our own on the STM32WL would remove it.

**25 Nov.** Shopping list and wiring drawings (the "Wio-E5 Star Kit" page). The ST-Link was sold out, so a Raspberry Pi Pico W became the SWD programmer: debugprobe 2.3.1 loaded, it shows up as CMSIS-DAP plus COM16, and ESP-IDF's OpenOCD drives it. It is for stage 3 (our own STM32WL firmware); nothing has been programmed with it yet.

## 1. Second gateway (995A) on the Wio-E5

- The spare ESP32-S31 had another project on it. Its ROM cannot erase the whole chip, so only the settings area (0x9000–0x20000) was erased before flashing the Wio gateway build.
- **The S31's USB-HS port powers a device in host mode.** Mini A plugged in with the OTG cable, lit up and was found as a CP2102N at 115200. That was the open hardware question.
- **Bug found: the web page had 22 handlers and room for 20.** The last two, **Reboot** and saving a survey, were silently never registered. This is in the E52 build too, so **9D44 has it until it is reflashed**. The limit is now sized from the handler table.
- **Bug found: start-up stopped after the radio was configured.** The radio task (priority 12) held the port lock almost all the time: it waited for incoming lines while holding it, released it and took it straight back. Lower-priority callers (start-up, the web AT console) never got in. It now sleeps one tick with the lock free. While there: it also busy-waited during an ack wait; it now blocks until the next deadline (at most 10 ms). That matters most on the single-core C6.

## 2. Laptop test node and the first star link

`tools/wionode.py` runs a Wio-E5 mini on a PC as an LMESH node speaking the star frame (same agent as `pcnode.py`: announce, ping/pong, Modbus simulator, MQTT).

- First link, on the mesh's channel 65 / network 0x1234: the gateway listed `wionode` at 0002, every frame acked on the first try (240–254 ms), ping round trip 440 ms.
- The node also heard 9D44's E52 frames on 915.125 MHz (E52 rate 1 is exactly LoRa SF7 at 500 kHz) and ignored them as "not a star frame", as designed.

## 3. A configuration mix-up, and how it was put right

- 995A's settings were entered on the phone, but the phone was on **9D44's** access point (both gateways answer at 192.168.4.1). 9D44 had its radio moved to channel 73 / network 0x2001.
- Restored on 9D44 to its 24 Nov values: channel 65, rate 1, 22 dBm, network 0x1234, address 1, CSMA 40, with Save + apply.
- **Checked over the air, not just on the page:** a Wio-E5 tuned to 915.125 MHz heard 9D44 (0001) exchanging frames with nodes 4FA4 and 22B1, both answering.
- 995A was then set from the laptop over the LAN: Wi-Fi `outlaw` (192.168.1.44), channel 73 (923.125 MHz), rate 1 (SF7), network 0x2001.

**Lesson:** set a gateway from the laptop over the LAN where possible. The two access points look identical once you are on one.

## 4. Gateways side by side: 8 MHz is not much

On channel 73, the test node also heard 9D44's frames, at −88 dBm instead of the −22 dBm they arrive at on their own channel. Their sequence numbers ran on from the ones heard on channel 65, so they came from one transmitter on 915.125 MHz, leaking 8 MHz across, about a metre away. Only 9D44's own frames came through, never its nodes'. Harmless here, because each side ignores the other's frames, but two gateways installed side by side would need more spacing or less power.

## 5. MQTT through 995A

- 995A publishes to Mosquitto on the laptop (192.168.1.20) under `lmesh/995A/…`; 9D44 uses `lmesh/9D44/…` on the same broker.
- Uplink: `gw/status online`, `0002/hello node up`, `0002/count 1…5`, every frame acked on the first try.
- Downlink: the first `set/led on` arrived **three times**. Not a gateway fault: a downlink has its own acknowledgement (an empty datagram back to port 1884, same seq), and `wionode.py` had copied `pcnode.py`, which never sent it. Both tools now ack like `node.c` and ignore resends. After the fix: `set/led on` and `set/setpoint 42.5` each delivered once.

## 6. Range walk

Both gateways inside the house, 995A on external USB power. Test node: mini B on the laptop, pinging every 5 s. Log: `docs/logs/range-2026-11-26.log`; summarised with `tools/rangelog.py`. Signal = the gateway's frames as heard at the node.

| Phase | Set-up | Pings OK | dBm, min / median | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 22 dBm both, no attenuator, inside and around the house (17:27–17:32) | 59 of 61 | −85 / −54 | Both losses while 995A was moved to its own power and rebooting |
| B | Attenuator on B, about 23 dB (17:33–17:35) | 14 of 15 | −70 / −51 | The loss was me trying power values on the gateway |
| C | Attenuator, both radios at −1 dBm: about 42 dB handicap (17:35–17:43) | 91 of 99 | −96 / −64 | Fine inside to 15 m; lost the moment B went outside (17:36:01–17:36:43) |
| D | Both at −1 dBm, no attenuator, outside (17:43–17:48) | 56 of 60 | −84 / −60 | Out to about 40 m; the 4 losses were short dead spots near 40 m |

What it says:
- **At −1 dBm, distance was not the limit.** At 40 m the median signal still had about 45 dB above the SF7 floor (about −117 dBm). What cut the link were particular spots where something solid blocked the path for 5–10 s.
- **The house wall is expensive.** With 42 dB of handicap, stepping outside lost everything, from −85 dBm inside. For a real install, the gateway antenna goes outside and up high.
- **The retry window is shorter than a dead spot.** A frame gets 4 tries over about 2.5 s; the dead spots lasted 5–10 s. Downlinks get 3 more tries from the gateway on top; a node's uplinks get nothing more. See open items.
- **Wio-E5 test mode takes −1 to 22 dBm**, not the E52's −9 to 22: −9 and −5 were refused with `ERROR(-1)` and the module stayed on its old settings without saying so. `wio.c` now clamps to −1.
- During phase C the gateway's counters showed it never heard the node in a failed ping. That does **not** show the downlink was fine at those moments: the gateway had nothing to send. I said otherwise at first.

## 7. First C6 field node on the star (7A44)

- **Mini C** was on factory settings (9600 baud, DevEui ending 51:2B). Set to 115200 and checked as test node 0003 (3 of 3 pings) **before** R3 came off, because afterwards the laptop cannot send it commands.
- **R3 removed.** The resistor that beeps to the plain RX pin (pin 19, across from a GND pin; not RX1 or RX2). Checked: only the pad on the pin-19 side still beeps to RX. R4 untouched. Marked NODE.
- **7A44** (XIAO C6; MAC ends 79:44, so its access point is `LMESH-NODE-7944`, while its radio address is 0x0100 + 0x7944 = 0x7A44): D4–D5 loopback wire removed, wired to mini C (5V–pin 1, GND–pin 24, D4–RX, D5–TX), Wio node build flashed.
- **Bug found (node only): every transmission "failed".** The module did report TX DONE on time, but `uart_read_bytes()` waits until it has the full count asked for (600 bytes), so every reply sat in the buffer until the caller's timeout ran out. The gateway reads over USB and never had this. Fixed: read whatever has arrived. Boot time fell from 11 s to 1.5 s.
- Radio set through its own access point from the laptop (radio settings are local-only by design): channel 73, network 0x2001, gateway 1.
- **Result:** the gateway lists `lmesh-node`, kind `rtu`, firmware 0.7.5, at −28 dBm. Gateway ping 447–580 ms, 5 of 5. MQTT `7A44/set/config {"site":""}` → `7A44/config/ack {"ok":true,"site":""}`, acked first time.

The whole star path has now worked with real hardware: broker → 995A → radio → C6 + mini C → and back.

## 9. Things I got wrong along the way

- Told you to set 995A on the phone without warning that both gateways' access points answer at the same address.
- During the range walk, said the downlink was fine during the dropouts. The counters only showed the gateway did not hear the node.
- Said the laptop was online over Ethernet while its Wi-Fi was on the node's access point. Neither Ethernet port had a link; it worked only because the whole switch happened in one step.
- Assumed the E52's −9 dBm minimum applied to the Wio-E5. It is −1.
- Looked for `LMESH-NODE-7A44`. The access point is named after the MAC (7944); 7A44 is the radio address.
- Pinged `"7A44"` as a string; the API read it as node 7. It takes the number (31300).
- Suspected the supply for 7A44's failed transmissions. It was the UART read.

## 12. Open items

- **Uplink retries on nodes.** Dead spots last 5–10 s; a frame's 4 tries cover about 2.5 s. Poll data and write acks from a node should retry over a longer window.
- **7A44's pings take about 120 ms longer than the laptop node's** (560 against 440 ms). Something on the node adds a delay.
- **Wio builds report the same firmware version as E52 builds** (gateway 1.0.0, node 0.7.5). Add a radio tag so the node list shows which radio a node has.
- **Pages still say E52:** "Apply to E52", rates labelled in kbps, power −9..22. Relabel for Wio builds.
- The gateway sends its radio settings three times at boot (harmless, about 1 s), and logs "E52 not responding" while it waits for the Wio-E5.
- **9D44** needs reflashing to get the Reboot button back.
- **Mini D** to check and set to 115200.
- **Stage 3:** Pico W to the Wio-E5 Dev Board. The unlock erases the AT firmware for good.

## 13. Before the outdoor test

1. **Antennas.** Gateway antenna outside and as high as you can, short coax. Never transmit without an antenna on.
2. **Power.** Both radios at 22 dBm: 995A and 7A44 already are. The launcher `range-walk.cmd` uses 22 dBm unless told otherwise.
3. **Keep 9D44 away from 995A,** or switch it off, so its leakage does not muddy the numbers.
4. **Logins.** Change the default passwords on 995A and 7A44 first.
5. **Order of runs.** SF7 (rate 1) first, then SF10 (rate 3) for reach. Changing rate means the gateway (its page, Radio → rate, then apply) and every node on the star: the test node with `--rate 3`, 7A44 only on its own page.
6. **Logging.** Run `range-walk.cmd` on the laptop, and record a GPS track on the phone so distance can be matched to the log by time. Afterwards: `python tools/rangelog.py <log> --every 30`.
7. **Expect** short dead spots behind solid objects: with the current retry window, those pings will be lost, and that is useful data.
8. **Hardening.** Solder or crimp the mini-to-XIAO wiring instead of jumpers, relieve the strain on the wires and connectors, weatherproof the SMA joints, and keep mini C's USB-C unplugged while it is wired to the XIAO.

Antenna gain and the rules: at 22 dBm into the radio, an antenna of up to about 14 dBi stays within 36 dBm EIRP. That is our reading of FCC 15.247 / RSS-247 for a 500 kHz signal; check it for your antenna before going higher.
